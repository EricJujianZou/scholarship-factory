## Honesty guardrails (claim boundary, standing rule 5)

- These were **customer-discovery interviews, not endorsements**. Ship them
  as "what recruiters and hiring managers told us in interviews," never as
  product testimonials. That framing is also stronger: it's authority
  validation of the method, which is what a skeptical reader actually
  doubts.
- The source files are the owner's **session notes, not verbatim
  transcripts**. Anything inside quotation marks on a shipped surface must
  be confirmed by the owner as faithful to what was said. Where wording is
  reconstructed, use reported speech ("A campus recruiter told us that...")
  instead of quote marks.
- Numbers below (1,000 applicants, 6 seconds, 20%) are what interviewees
  reported about THEIR process — attribute them to the speaker, never state
  them as UGMI data.
- Roles only, no names, no current-employer pairings specific enough to
  identify (e.g. "senior tech recruiter", not "senior recruiter at Lyft").

## Tier 1 — page candidates (validate the exact mechanism we sell)

1. **"Apply, and then email the recruiter that you applied — that's how you
   get picked up out of the ATS."** — campus recruiter at a large employer.
   THE quote: a recruiter prescribing our core product mechanic
   word-for-word. (Muqdas)
2. **"Referrals are the best way in."** — PM at a major semiconductor
   company (Jacob). Reinforced by: "Most of our hires come from referrals."
   — hiring manager (Harry).
3. **"Sometimes the keywords the AI screens for are so odd and trivial that
   a resume gets rejected without ever being looked at."** — hiring manager
   who uses AI screening (Harry). Pairs with: "The ATS isn't chronological —
   it surfaces resumes by keyword match, basically at random." — campus
   recruiter (Muqdas).
4. **"Two hours to review a pool of a thousand applicants — you get through
   maybe 20%."** — senior tech recruiter (Alina). The honest version of
   "most applications are never read."
5. **"A hundred resumes in twenty minutes. Six seconds each."** — hiring
   lead (Fred). Also his: "Your resume isn't who you are, it's why I should
   hire you."
6. **"Don't spray and pray. Pick the few companies you actually want,
   research them deeply, build something relevant, and reach out to them
   directly."** — startup founder (Kamil). Validates the anti-mass-apply
   pitch AND the $200 pilot's method.
7. **"When you limit your applications, you choose better. A resume tells me
   nothing about you as an individual."** — startup founder hiring
   engineers (Chris). Also his: cover letters with a personal touch stand
   out.

## Tier 2 — content/DM ammo (pain and urgency, better in reels than on page)

- 1,000 applications in one week for a single posting. (game studio founder)
- Generic entry-level postings pull hundreds to thousands; the team
  pursues the top 5 per day. (engineering manager, Faisal)
- Summer posting goes up in December — you need ~4 months of lead time.
  "Apply early is true." (Muqdas; Fred)
- Master's and PhD students are now applying for internships — the
  oversaturation is real. (Muqdas)
- "If a resume reads AI-generated, I skip it." (director, James) — pairs
  with: they don't hire for technical skill at co-op level; personality,
  care, teamwork. (Fred)
- After an interview, follow up by email with why you provide value —
  recruiters notice. (Muqdas)
- Even reaching the interview stage, an agency sends 40–60 candidates per
  role. (agency recruiter, Nina)
- Recruiter cold-emails to engineers dried up a year and a half ago —
  nobody is coming to find you. (Jacob)
- Interview processes run 1–1.5 months with multiple rounds — the pipeline
  is slow even when it works. (Muqdas, Kelly)
